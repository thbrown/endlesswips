Baby Tips: Packing for a Toddler

An exhaustive, err exhausting, list

First Posted: October 17, 2023
Last Updated: October 17, 2023

#Toddlers

Toddlers need a lot of stuff! Here's a relatively comprehensive list of things I consider bringing when we go anywhere overnight, from Grandma & Grandpa's house, to Disney World, to visit family a few states away. You don't need to bring everything on this list every time, but an exhaustive list helps you remember what you might be forgetting.

#Overview

Using packing cubes, this is how I typically pack:

  1. 1 L cube of shirts/pants, per kid
  2. 1 M cube of everyone's nighttime stuff
  3. 1 S cube of socks/underwear/shoes, per kid
  4. (If applicable) 1 M cube with the whole family's swim stuff
  5. 1 M cube of everything else - overflow shoes, jackets, hats, mittens, sunglasses
  6. 1 M cube in the diaper bag with extra clothes/jackets/hats/sunglasses
  7. 1 M cube/gallon Ziploc in the diaper bag with snacks

Consider just putting everything on a table / bed until you have everything you need in that place. Then actually pack the things in your bags; otherwise, you may forget whether you packed something.

#Clothing

First, count how many days you'll be there. Then, check the weather.

  1. Weather appropriate clothing
    • Shirts - long-sleeved, short-sleeved
    • Bottoms - pants or shorts
    • An extra or two of each, depending on your laundry options and your toddler's proclivity to messiness
  2. PJs
    • Consider whether the sleeping room will be colder/warmer than the rest of the weather (e.g., high A/C in hotels, the people you're visiting keep their house at temperatures you're not used to)
    • An extra or two, unless you can convince your toddler to wear regular clothes to bed instead of PJs if you run out
  3. Socks / Underwear
    • They're tiny - bring a few extras. But also, they can often be re-worn if necessary.
  4. Jackets
    • Usually like to have one in the diaper bag
    • Think about rain jackets vs. warm jackets (hoods? no hoods?)
  5. Shoes
    • I usually pack at least 2 pairs of shoes (including the ones they wear when we leave the house) in case they get wet or muddy
    • Consider setting aside shoes for travel day ahead of time so they don't get lost
    • Consider whether formal shoes or shoes that can get wet are needed

#Optional

  1. Hats
    • Sun hats or winter hats
  2. Mittens/Gloves
    • Waterproof? Just warm?
  3. Swimsuit
    • Swim diaper options (cloth, disposable); definitely want more than one
    • On a beach/pool vacation, I like to bring two cloth swim diapers (we have these) and two swimsuits so that when one inevitably isn't quite dry yet, you have a dry one to put on

#Diaper Bag

I typically restock the diaper bag with a few things that we don't always have in there when we're not traveling:

  1. Diapers (~5 ish)
  2. Refill wipes container
  3. Snacks
  4. Hand sanitizer
  5. Sanitizing wipes (for germs, not for bottoms)
  6. Ibuprofen for adults
  7. Water bottles
  8. Extra clothes/jackets/hats/sunglasses we might need before being able to unpack the main suitcase
  9. Toys/books for airplane ride

#Health

#Brushing

  1. Toothbrush
  2. Toothpaste

#Bath

  1. Baby soap
  2. Baby lotion
  3. Washcloth (optional - will the place you're bathing provide a washcloth?)

#Bottom

  1. Diapers - as many as you need on the trip (we typically bring them in the car seat, so there is space and we don't have to find a place to buy them when we get there)
  2. Wipes - same
  3. Diaper cream

#Other

  1. Sunscreen

#Sick Items

If your kiddo is prone to sickness, you might want some of these things with you.

  1. Thermometer
  2. Tylenol/ibuprofen
    • You can bring this on as a carry-on even above 100ml, just declare it to TSA
  3. Syringes / cups to administer liquid medication
  4. Extra tissues
  5. Nose Frida, if applicable
  6. Saline spray/drops

#Eating

  1. Water bottle(s)
  2. Straw cup, or something the kid can drink milk out of without you worrying about spilling; easy to clean. We love these.
  3. Kid sized utensils (if they can't use adult ones in a pinch; otherwise save yourself bringing one more thing)
  4. Bib (just one silicone one should do)
  5. Booster/high chair, if really needed
    • These are usually big. Try not to bring them - can your toddler just sit on your lap? Or get a travel-sized one.

#Snacks

Our kids' diets are not awesome on trips. And we've decided that's okay. Some favorites:

  1. Dried fruit
  2. Granola bars
  3. Fruit snacks
  4. Goldfish (in a container they have to stick their hand in so it won't spill)
  5. Nuts
  6. PB&Js (for the day of travel)

#Playing

  1. Book(s) - usually 2-3 is fine; whatever you need for bedtime
  2. Toys - one or two that are easy to clean
    • If going on an airplane, consider a tablet and headphones
    • We try to bring a selection of mess-free drawing supplies
  3. Stuffed animals - we let our kids each pick a small snuggly to bring for bedtime/the airplane

#Sleeping

Many of these are optional; your toddler may or may not use them.

  1. Pack n Play sheets
  2. Sleep sack
  3. Snuggly
  4. Blanket
  5. Night light / sound machine / OK to wake light
  6. Monitor
    • We don't always bring our monitor, but if we want to spend a good chunk of awake time outside of earshot from where the baby's sleeping, or if we expect they'll have a hard time settling, a video monitor can be nice. Consider whether yours can connect to a Wifi network in an unfamiliar place.

#Transportation

  1. Baby Carrier
  2. Car seat
    • See Traveling with Baby
  3. Stroller
    • Do you really need one wherever you're going? Could you use a smaller stroller? Can you just take turns carrying them when they don't want to walk?

#Misc

These may or may not apply:

  1. Pool/beach toys
  2. Pool/beach towels
  3. Travel laundry detergent
  4. Dish soap to wash kid stuff
  5. Gallon/sandwich plastic bags
  6. Plastic grocery bags

Extra bags take up very little space and are handy for all sorts of things while traveling.

#Final Check

Now that you've packed everything thrown everything in a pile, run through a few important moments in your head:

  1. Bedtime
    • Do you have everything you need to perform your routine to the satisfaction of your picky toddler?
  2. Mealtime
    • Are you missing something that's easy to pack that will make life way easier?
  3. Inclement weather
    • If it is likely to rain/snow, will you be sad you didn't pack something to make it smoother?
  4. Special places you're going
    • Pool, beach, park, hike, boat, wedding, Disney World
